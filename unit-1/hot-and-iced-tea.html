Why the word "average" matters

Not every particle in a drink moves at the same speed. They are always bumping into each other, so some speed up and some slow down. Press Spot the surprise and you will find a particle in the iced tea that is moving faster than a particle in the hot tea.

So a thermometer cannot be measuring one particle. It measures the average. The particles of the tea hit the thermometer until its own particles move at the same average speed, and the reading tells you that average.

Three Words That Sound Alike

WordWhat it meansHot tea vs iced tea
Kinetic energyThe energy of motion. One particle moving faster has more kinetic energy.Each particle has its own, and it keeps changing.
TemperatureA measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles. This is what a thermometer reads.Hot tea is higher: its particles move faster on average.
Thermal energyThe total kinetic energy of all the particles added together. More particles means more to add up.Depends on the temperature and on how much tea there is.
Misconception alert: "Cold things have no thermal energy"

Look at the iced tea. Its particles are slower, but they never stop. Even ice is full of vibrating particles. Particles would only stop moving at absolute zero, about −273°C, which is why the iced tea's energy bar is lower than the hot tea's but still far from empty.

The big idea

A thermometer tells you how fast the particles are moving on average. It does not tell you how much tea there is.

Two drinks can have the same temperature and very different thermal energy. A swimming pool at 25°C has far more thermal energy than a cup of tea at 80°C, because it has so many more particles to add up.
