What a CER Is

You will write one CER for each of your two focus areas in the Unit 1 Summative. This page holds the same sentence frames and word bank as your handout, but it checks your writing as you go and saves it in your browser.

PartIn shortWhat it does
ClaimYour answer.A short statement that directly answers your focus-area question about your phenomenon.
EvidenceYour proof.Specific details from your model, diagrams, or observations that back up your claim.
ReasoningYour "so what."The science explanation that connects your evidence to your claim, and why the evidence proves it is true.

1. Claim

Why this part matters: Your claim is the specific answer to your focus-area question. It should be clear enough that someone could check it against your model.

Write your claim 1 to 2 sentences

Pick the level that helps you most. Click a frame to drop it into your writing. You can change the words, mix levels, or write your own.

Avoid: Do not just restate the question, and do not describe the whole phenomenon in general. A claim is ONE specific, checkable answer, not a summary.

2. Evidence

Why this part matters: Evidence is your proof: exact details from your model, such as particle spacing, motion, arrows, and labels, that support your claim. Vague statements like "it looks cool" are not evidence.

Write your evidence 3 to 5 sentences

Pick the level that helps you most. Click a frame to drop it into your writing. You can change the words, mix levels, or write your own.

Avoid: Do not just repeat your claim in different words. Evidence must point to something specific in your model or observations: particle spacing, particle motion, forces, or energy direction.

3. Reasoning

Why this part matters: Reasoning connects your evidence back to the science ideas and explains WHY the evidence proves your claim. This is the part students skip most often, so do not skip it.

Write your reasoning 3 to 5 sentences

Pick the level that helps you most. Click a frame to drop it into your writing. You can change the words, mix levels, or write your own.

Avoid: Do not just repeat your evidence again. Reasoning has to explain WHY, using a science idea such as particle motion, spacing, intermolecular forces, or energy.

Annotated Example

Show the worked example: water boiling in a kettle

Phenomenon: Water boiling in a kettle  |  Focus area: Thermal energy change

Claim

In water boiling in a kettle, the water particles move faster and spread farther apart because thermal energy is being added.

Evidence

In my model, I drew the liquid water particles close together and moving at a medium speed before heating. After heating, I drew the particles as gas (steam) far apart and moving very fast, with arrows showing energy entering the pot. The bubbles in my model show the liquid changing to gas as it reaches its boiling point.

Reasoning

This evidence supports my claim because, according to the kinetic molecular theory, adding thermal energy increases the kinetic energy of particles, causing them to move faster and spread farther apart. This is why the tightly-packed liquid water particles become widely-spaced gas particles (steam), since the added energy overcomes the intermolecular forces holding the liquid particles together. As a result, water changes state from liquid to gas.

Notice that each part does a different job. The claim states the answer. The evidence points to exact details in the model. The reasoning explains why the evidence proves the claim, using particle motion, spacing, forces, and energy, rather than repeating the evidence.
