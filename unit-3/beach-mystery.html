Look at the beakers before you heat them. Every beaker holds 100 g, but they are not the same size. Iron is so dense that 100 g is a small block.

Same energy in, different temperature rise

The burners were identical and the time was the same, so both beakers got the same amount of energy. Yet the temperatures went up by very different amounts.

Remember that temperature measures how fast the particles move on average. Some substances need a lot of energy to make their particles speed up. Others need only a little. Water needs a lot. Iron needs very little.

Specific Heat

Specific heat: the amount of energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 gram of a substance by 1°C.

SubstanceSpecific heat (J per g per °C)What that means
Water4.18Very high. Slow to heat up, slow to cool down.
Cooking oilabout 2.0About half of water.
Sandabout 0.8About one fifth of water. Heats up fast in the sun.
Iron0.45Very low. A pan gets hot in seconds.

A joule (J) is a unit of energy. You do not need to calculate anything here. Just notice the pattern: the higher the specific heat, the smaller the temperature rise for the same energy.

Back to the beach

The sun gives the sand and the sea about the same energy. Sand has a low specific heat, so its temperature shoots up. Water has a high specific heat, so the sea warms only a little. After sunset it flips: the sand loses its heat quickly and cools down, while the sea stays mild all night.

The big idea

How much an object's temperature goes up depends on three things:

  • How much energy is added
  • How much mass there is: more mass means more particles to share the energy
  • What the substance is: its specific heat

That is why a full kettle takes longer to boil than a half-full one, and why the sea stays cool while the sand burns.

Misconception alert: "Hotter means it got more energy"

In Lab 1 the sand got much hotter than the water, but both got exactly the same energy from identical burners. A bigger temperature rise does not mean more energy went in. It means that substance needs less energy per degree.
